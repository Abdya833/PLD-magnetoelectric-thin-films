

# Growth and Characterization of Magnetoelectric Bilayers Using Pulsed Laser Deposition (PLD)

## 1. Project Overview

This project focused on the growth and characterization of magnetoelectric bilayer thin films using Pulsed Laser Deposition (PLD). The study explored the relationship between thin-film growth, structural properties, dielectric response, and the potential for interfacial coupling between the constituent functional layers.

Magnetoelectric heterostructures are of interest for multifunctional electronic devices because they can enable coupling between magnetic and electric properties. In bilayer systems, this coupling can arise through mechanisms such as strain transfer, interfacial interactions, and magnetoelastic effects, depending on the materials and device architecture.

The project involved thin-film deposition and materials characterization to investigate the properties of the fabricated bilayer structure.

## 2. Project Objectives

- Fabricate magnetoelectric bilayer thin films using Pulsed Laser Deposition.
- Investigate the structural characteristics of the deposited films using X-ray Diffraction (XRD).
- Examine surface morphology and microstructural features using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM).
- Characterize dielectric properties using dielectric spectroscopy.
- Understand how interfaces and structural characteristics can influence coupling between functional layers.

## 3. Deposition Technique: Pulsed Laser Deposition

Pulsed Laser Deposition is a physical vapour deposition technique in which a high-energy pulsed laser ablates a solid target. The resulting plume of energetic species travels toward a substrate, where material condenses to form a thin film.

Important process variables in PLD include:

- Laser fluence and pulse frequency
- Target-to-substrate distance
- Substrate temperature
- Background gas pressure and composition
- Deposition time and target composition

These parameters can influence film growth, crystallinity, surface morphology, composition, and interfacial quality.

## 4. Experimental and Characterization Workflow

### 4.1 Thin-Film Deposition

Magnetoelectric bilayer thin films were grown using PLD. The technique enables controlled deposition of functional oxide films and provides a route to fabricate layered structures for investigating coupled material properties.

### 4.2 X-Ray Diffraction (XRD)

XRD was used to investigate the structural characteristics of the deposited films.

The technique provides information about:
- Crystalline phases and phase purity
- Crystallographic orientations and preferred texture
- Structural changes that may be associated with film growth conditions

Phase identification and interpretation require comparison of the measured diffraction pattern with appropriate reference data.

### 4.3 Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)

SEM was used to examine surface morphology and microstructural features of the deposited films.

Surface morphology can provide useful information about film continuity, grain-like features, surface defects, and deposition-related growth characteristics. Interpretation depends on the image resolution and the available cross-sectional or surface images.

### 4.4 Dielectric Spectroscopy

Dielectric spectroscopy was used to characterize the dielectric response of the material system.

Depending on the measurement configuration and available data, dielectric measurements can be analysed as a function of frequency and temperature to investigate polarization-related behaviour, dielectric dispersion, and possible contributions from interfaces.

## 5. Scientific Background: Magnetoelectric Coupling

Magnetoelectric coupling refers to the interaction between electric and magnetic properties in a material or composite structure.

In bilayer heterostructures, coupling can occur through different mechanisms:

- **Strain-mediated coupling:** Mechanical strain generated in one layer influences the functional response of the adjacent layer.
- **Interfacial coupling:** Interactions at the interface influence the properties of the constituent layers.
- **Magnetoelastic effects:** Coupling between magnetic behaviour and mechanical strain contributes to the overall response.

Structural characterization and dielectric measurements help investigate the properties of the fabricated system. However, demonstrating magnetoelectric coupling conclusively requires appropriate measurements that directly establish the relationship between electric and magnetic responses.
